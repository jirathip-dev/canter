## 1. The defect

The issue reproduces a red `staging`: the fan-out frontier witness failed
against **its own ~60 s bound** (`61.17s`) while the product path was fine.
The run's driver wakes semantically on each committed step and otherwise
re-checks on its bounded timer fallback — `supervision::DEFAULT_CHECK_INTERVAL_SECS`
= 60 s — so a starved wake on a loaded host makes the witness red-or-green by
luck, and a timing margin becomes a merge blocker.

Reproduced in this lane's own probe runs: the stuck-frontier probe below shows
the exact shape (`next_step: "p2-5"`, `next_check.due_in_secs: 55`,
`in_flight: ""`, `observed.eligible: true`) — the frontier resting on the
driver's fallback tick.

## 2. The fix (test-only; no product behaviour changes)

Every *frontier* wait is now progress-driven, with an explicit, documented
deadlock bound:

- `FRONTIER_NO_PROGRESS_SECS = 240` (seconds). The wait fails only after 240 s
  in which the recorded cursor did not change; any new recorded attempt, a
  settlement or a frontier move resets the ceiling. A re-read of an unchanged
  cursor — however many driver checks were committed in between — is not
  progress, so a genuinely stuck frontier still fails instead of hanging until
  the driver kills the suite.
- Why 240: four driver ticks. One (or three) starved wakes can never fail the
  witness, and 240 s stays comfortably inside the CI test driver's per-suite
  budget (`scripts/ci-test-driver.py`, `PER_SUITE_SECONDS = 300`, serial), so
  the failure is the witness's own typed panic naming its progress, never the
  driver's kill. No assertion depends on a margin of a second.
- The failure message states the progress observed (frontier, number of
  recorded attempts, in-flight step) and the elapsed time.
- Progress signature: `canonical_text(cursor)` — frontier, attempt ledger and
  in-flight step in one canonical rendering.
